The Big O

Introduction

Pleasure is intimate, ordinary, and surprisingly hard to describe. It can be felt alone or with someone else. It can be anticipated, interrupted, or never spoken about at all.

We often talk about sex as though pleasure naturally follows, and as though everyone means the same thing when they say it does. But what do we actually know about how people experience it?

Researchers have tried to turn private experiences into numbers. Those numbers can reveal patterns, though they cannot tell the whole story. Let’s begin with one of them.

*This project uses categories reported in one study, not a complete list of identities. Its results describe the study's participants; they do not predict your experience.

Frederick et al. (2018) reported orgasm-frequency results for six groups: heterosexual, lesbian, and bisexual women; and heterosexual, gay, and bisexual men. The paper calls its comparison one of gender and sexual orientation, but does not give the exact wording of the survey's sex or gender question. It does not report a result for every identity. The figures describe responses from this study's participants, not any individual's likelihood of orgasm.
