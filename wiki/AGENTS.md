# Research wiki workflow

This directory is an agent-maintained navigation and synthesis layer for the student's project. Follow the repository's root `AGENTS.md` as well. The student controls the creative direction and reviews interpretations; this wiki does not set a thesis or prescribe visual design.

## Layers

- **Published sources:** Papers and editorial references are linked from `research/sources.md`. Most full texts are not stored in this repository. Do not treat a local summary as a substitute for the paper.
- **Source-derived project records:** `research/notes/` records one source at a time; `data/raw/` contains manually transcribed aggregate values, not original participant data. Preserve their provenance and study boundaries.
- **Wiki:** Files here connect sources, measures, questions, and experiments. They are agent-written working notes and may be revised after review. Never silently replace student writing or creative decisions elsewhere.

## When a source or experiment changes

1. Read the relevant original source, `research/sources.md`, its source note, `data/README.md`, and any affected experiment README. Record retrieval status if source access is limited.
2. Update the affected wiki page with links to the specific source note, CSV, table/figure, or experiment. Separate **observed result**, **interpretation**, and **open question**. Do not invent missing values or infer causation from associations.
3. Add or adjust cross-links in `index.md`. Append a dated entry to `log.md` describing what changed and what remains unverified.
4. Check for conflicting outcome definitions, unsupported claims, dead local links, and pages missing from the index. Resolve factual errors against primary sources; flag unresolved conflicts rather than smoothing them over.

## Querying and maintaining the wiki

- Start at `index.md`, then read the linked pages and underlying source records before answering a research question.
- Cite the original publication and the local note/CSV when reporting a number. Clearly label any inference.
- Keep study samples and outcomes separate: orgasm frequency, event-level occurrence, enjoyment, technique use, sexual satisfaction, and relationship satisfaction are different measures.
- Add a new page only when a recurring topic needs one. The index can remain small. No additional wiki software or dependency is required.
- Do not copy full copyrighted articles or transcripts into the wiki.
