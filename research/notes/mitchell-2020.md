# Mitchell et al. (2020)

**Citation and access.** Virginia E. Mitchell, Justin K. Mogilski, Sarah H. Donaldson, Sylis Claire A. Nicolas, and Lisa L. M. Welling, “Sexual Motivation and Satisfaction Among Consensually Non-Monogamous and Monogamous Individuals,” *The Journal of Sexual Medicine* 17(6), 1072–1085 (2020), [DOI 10.1016/j.jsxm.2020.02.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsxm.2020.02.018). Mogilski](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340071027_Sexual_Motivation_and_Satisfaction_Among_Consensually_Non-Monogamous_and_Monogamous_Individuals) consulted 2026-11-02. **Question and design.** Observational comparison of sexual motives and sexual satisfaction across relationship configurations, with additional analysis of multiple partners in CNM relationships. Recruitment came from a university and online forums used by CNM participants. Analyses used multivariate ANOVA and hierarchical regressions.

**Sample and measures.** Final N = 449: monogamous n = 322; non-exclusive relationship with one partner n = 40; non-exclusive relationship with more than one partner n = 87. The author-uploaded text reports 83 men, 357 women, and 9 participants classified as “other,” with mean age 23.15 years. The Why Humans Have Sex Scale measures motives. The New Scale for Sexual Satisfaction asks about satisfaction with sex life over the preceding six months on a 1–5 scale, with ego-focused and partner/activity-focused subscales. Table 1 describes participant demographics. Outcome estimates and uncertainty still need a careful table-level transcription before charting.

**Observed findings.** The abstract reports stronger physical motives among the multipartner non-exclusive group than the monogamous group and no significant sexual-satisfaction difference in the stated comparison of monogamous participants with the multipartner group’s primary and secondary partners. No numerical outcome values were available for extraction.

**Limits.** Small CNM groups and targeted recruitment in self-identified CNM online forums limit generalizability. The author-uploaded text should be compared with the version of record for any value used in a final graphic. Motives, sexual satisfaction, and orgasm frequency are distinct constructs. The results do not support causal claims about configuration.
