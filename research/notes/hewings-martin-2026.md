# Hewings-Martin et al. (2026): solo and partnered activities

- **Source:** Yella Hewings-Martin, Adam C. Cunningham, Jordan Rullo, Karen L. Blair, Carley Prentice, Anna Klepchukova, and Liudmila Zhaunova, “Sexual Satisfaction and Orgasm Experiences During Partnered and Solo Sex Among 27,931 Users of the Flo App,” *Archives of Sexual Behavior* 55, 2483–2503 (published online June 9, 2026). [Open article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-025-03393-y), [publisher PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10508-025-03393-y.pdf). Accessed November 6, 2026.
- **Sample and collection:** English-language Flo app users over 18, invited in-app April 11–May 5, 2024. Of 70,424 responses, 27,931 met completion, app ID, and attention-check criteria. The sample was 99% female and 96% women; mean age 28.14. This is an app user sample, not a probability sample of all adults.
- **Activity question:** For partnered activities, “When you’re engaging in sexual activity with a partner, how often do you do the following?” For solo activities, “When you’re engaging in sexual activities on your own (solo), how often do you do the following?” Participants reported activity over the past six months on a 0–4 scale from never to every time.
- **Orgasm question:** “Over the past 6 months, when you had sexual stimulation or intercourse, how often did you reach orgasm (climax)?” Overall options were coded 0 = no sexual activity; 1 = almost never or never; 2 = a few times (less than half the time); 3 = sometimes (about half the time); 4 = most times (more than half the time); 5 = almost always or always. The same options were used for orgasm frequency from each activity participants reported engaging in.
- **Extracted data:** [Table 5](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10508-025-03393-y.pdf), printed page 2497 (PDF page 15), manually transcribed into `data/raw/hewings-martin-2026-paired-activities.csv`. Each row compares a pair of similar but sometimes nonidentical activities among participants with responses for both; each row has its own `n`. Mean values are **0–5 response scores, not percentages or orgasm probabilities**. Reported Cohen's d signs follow the article's partnered-minus-solo comparison. The paper reports `p < .001` for every Table 5 pair. Confidence intervals are not supplied in Table 5.
- **Other usable numbers:** The paper directly asked which context produced orgasm more frequently: 47% said solo, 21% partnered, and 27% about the same. These sum to 95%, apparently because of another response category or nonresponse; do not normalize or force a 100% split. Table 2 reports activity *engagement*, a separate outcome. Figure 3 shows mean orgasm scores by 13 activities in each context, but it does not provide machine-readable values; do not digitize without noting uncertainty.
- **Interpretation limits:** Respondents selected into both the app and survey. The comparison pairs differ in some cases: partner clitoral stimulation versus self stimulation with a vibrator is not the same action with only context changed. This is observational self-report, so it does not show that toys or any activity caused more orgasms. Overall sexual satisfaction is a different measure. Raw respondent data and analysis code are available only by request with Flo's permission, according to the article's data availability statement.
