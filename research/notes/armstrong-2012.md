# Armstrong, England & Fogarty (2012)

**Citation and access.** Elizabeth A. Armstrong, Paula England, and Alison C. K. Fogarty, “Accounting for Women’s Orgasm and Sexual Enjoyment in College Hookups and Relationships,” *American Sociological Review* 77(3), 435–462 (2012), [DOI 10.1177/0003122412445802](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122412445802). Public journal HTML accessed 2026-11-02.

**Question and design.** The authors ask how orgasm occurrence and sexual enjoyment vary between heterosexual women’s college hookups and relationship sexual events, and how practices, partner-specific experience, commitment, and gender norms relate to the difference. Methods combine an online undergraduate survey at 21 U.S. colleges (2005–2011) with 85 in-depth interviews at two universities.

**Sample and definitions.** Quantitative analysis starts with 14,583 undergraduate women; 13,484 remained after excluding respondents outside the study’s heterosexual focus. Overlapping analytic subsets had 6,881 complete cases reporting a most recent hookup with a man and 6,591 reporting a most recent sexual event in a relationship of at least six months with a man. The intercourse subsets in Table 1 had n = 2,748 and n = 5,242. The survey let respondents use their own definition of “hookup”; a relationship event went beyond kissing. Hookups and relationship events were distinct reported events, and the samples overlap.

**Measures.** Orgasm is a yes/no report for the event. Enjoyment asks how much the respondent enjoyed what happened physically; “very much” is the top response category. These should not be collapsed into one metric. The authors also record sexual practices, previous hookups with the same partner, and relationship indicators.

**Observed findings.** Table 1 reports orgasm proportions of .21 for all hookups, .42 for hookups with intercourse, .67 for relationship events, and .74 for relationship events with intercourse. The corresponding “very much” enjoyment proportions are .50, .59, .81, and .83. Figure 1/results text reports orgasm in 11% of first hookups, 16% of second or third hookups, and 34% of higher-order hookups. Values transcribed to `data/raw/armstrong-2012.csv`; Table 1 proportions were multiplied by 100 to express percent. Figure subgroup denominators remain unverified.

**Interpretation and limits.** The regression and interview findings suggest practices and familiarity matter, but an event-context association is not proof that commitment causes orgasm. The sample is college women and not a national probability sample; the paper focuses on women reporting male partners and omits other identities from its quantitative analysis. The authors note possible ambiguity in the hookup definition and selection into participating classes. See Table 1, Figure 1, Tables 2–4, and footnotes 6–8 for source detail.
