# Hensel et al. (2021): Pairing techniques

- **Source:** Devon J. Hensel, Christiana D. von Hippel, Charles C. Lapage, and Robert H. Perkins, “Women's techniques for making vaginal penetration more pleasurable: Results from a nationally representative study of adult women in the United States,” *PLOS ONE* 16(4), e0249242 (April 14, 2021). [Open article](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0249242), [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8046227/). Accessed November 6, 2026.
- **Sample and collection:** Second OMGYES Pleasure Report; U.S. probability web panel of 3,017 women ages 18–93. The panel was surveyed July 12–31, 2018, with weighting and an oversample of lesbian and bisexual women. Table 6's analyzed denominator is 2,877, not the full 3,017; the article says missing item responses were excluded.
- **Exact Pairing measure:** Participants answered yes/no to whether they had ever used each form of clitoral touch during vaginal penetration to “orgasm more often or have more pleasurable sex” than penetration alone. Self touch (finger or toy/vibrator) was termed *Solo Pairing*; touch by a partner (finger or toy/vibrator) was termed *Partner Pairing*. **Solo Pairing does not mean solo sex**; the term identifies who stimulates the clitoris during penetration. The question combines orgasm frequency and pleasure, so an affirmative answer cannot be treated as a measured increase in orgasm frequency.
- **Extracted data:** [Table 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8046227/#pone.0249242.t006) manually transcribed into `data/raw/hensel-2021-pairing.csv`. Values are weighted prevalence percentages and the reported counts, with N=2,877. Subforms overlap: a participant may have used more than one, so these rows must not be summed. Confidence intervals are not reported in Table 6.
- **Further data:** The authors state that the study's raw files are available from [ICPSR](https://doi.org/10.3886/E128421V2). I have not downloaded or reanalyzed them. Tables 3–5 in the paper separately cover Rocking and Shallowing with fingers, toys, penis, tongue/lips, as well as self-reported pleasure and orgasm changes; these could be extracted as a distinct next step.
- **Interpretation limits:** This is a retrospective, self-reported lifetime technique measure, not an experiment or an event-level orgasm rate. The published sample is women in the United States. The sponsor had a role in study design, data collection and analysis, and publication, as disclosed by the paper.
