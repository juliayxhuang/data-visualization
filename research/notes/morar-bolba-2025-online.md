# Morar-Bolba, Banse & Conley (online 2025; issue 2026)

**Citation and access.** Dora Morar-Bolba, Rainer Banse, and Terri D. Conley, “Consensual Non-Monogamy (CNM) - Considering Sexual Minorities and Moving Away from a Dyadic Conceptualization,” *International Journal of Sexual Health* 38(1), 337–356 (2026), [DOI 10.1080/19317611.2025.2576764](https://doi.org/10.1080/19317611.2025.2576764). Online October 24, 2025. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42016775/) and [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094264/) consulted 2026-11-02.

**Question and design.** Online German sample comparing relationship outcomes across monogamous, open, polyamorous, and swinger relationships while considering heterosexual and LGBQ participants. The abstract reports one-way ANOVAs and hierarchical regressions.

**Sample and measures.** The full text reports online recruitment from German community and CNM groups, beginning with 1,973 respondents and ending with N = 1,623 after eligibility and other exclusions. Table 1 gives monogamous n = 852, open relationship n = 140, polyamorous n = 501, and swinger n = 130. These counts are in `data/raw/morar-bolba-2025-online.csv`. Outcomes include relationship satisfaction, sexual satisfaction, and jealousy, along with proposed predictors of relationship satisfaction. Exact item wording, outcome scales, figure values, and confidence intervals still need extraction before charting.

**Observed findings.** The abstract reports that relationship satisfaction differed by relationship type only among LGBQ individuals, with the monogamous group slightly less satisfied than the polyamorous group. Among heterosexual participants, sexual satisfaction was lower in the monogamous group than CNM groups. These are qualitative descriptions of reported statistical comparisons; no numeric outcome table was extracted.

**Limits.** The paper explicitly notes that random sampling was infeasible, and that all non-heterosexual participants were combined as LGBQ in analysis despite differences within that category. The German context may differ from the North American studies that inform much of the literature. The observational design does not resolve selection effects or the causal direction of satisfaction and relationship choices. CNM subtypes should remain visible rather than being collapsed for convenience.
