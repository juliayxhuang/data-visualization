# Frederick et al. (2018)

**Citation and access.** David A. Frederick, H. Kate St. John, Justin R. Garcia, and Elisabeth A. Lloyd, “Differences in Orgasm Frequency Among Gay, Lesbian, Bisexual, and Heterosexual Men and Women in a U.S. National Sample,” *Archives of Sexual Behavior* 47, 273–288 (2018), [DOI 10.1007/s10508-017-0939-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-017-0939-z). Published online February 17, 2017. Publisher abstract accessed 2026-11-02. Student-supplied full PDF at `/Users/julia/Downloads/frederick.pdf` inspected 2026-11-02; it is not copied into the repository.

**Question and design.** Secondary observational analysis of anonymous responses to a survey posted on the NBC News website for ten days. The outcome item on printed page 276 asks, “During the past month, how often did [you]/[your partner] reach orgasm when you and he or she were intimate?” The five-point response scale runs from Never to Always. For some descriptive comparisons, the authors grouped responses as Never–Rarely, About Half of the Time, and Usually–Always; regressions used the continuous response. Participants reporting no intimacy in the past month were excluded. The authors call their comparison one of *gender* and sexual orientation and report men/women groups, but the paper does not give the survey's exact sex or gender question or its response options. We cannot determine from the paper whether it asked sex assigned at birth, current gender identity, or an unspecified male/female item. The study also tests associations with more than 30 traits or behaviors.

**Sample.** U.S. adults aged 18–65 who completed the survey through the NBC News entry portal, were married, remarried, cohabiting, or dating/seeing one person, and reported intimacy in the past month. N = 52,588: heterosexual men 26,032; gay men 452; bisexual men 550; lesbian women 340; bisexual women 1,112; heterosexual women 24,102 (printed Table 1, page 276). These are the authors’ reported categories. This is a self-selected website sample, not a probability sample of U.S. adults. Partner gender was not collected in this dataset.

**Observed findings.** The article abstract and Figure 1 (printed page 281) report the share saying they “usually–always” orgasmed: 95%, 89%, 88%, 86%, 66%, and 65%, respectively, in the category order above. These six observations are in `data/raw/frederick-2018.csv`. The paper also reports associations between certain practices and higher frequency among women; these are correlations, not causal estimates.

**Definitions and limits.** “When sexually intimate” is not necessarily the same measure as orgasm during intercourse, orgasm in the last event, or sexual satisfaction. The one-item outcome and self-selection are limitations the authors discuss on printed page 284. Confidence intervals for the six rounded abstract/Figure 1 percentages have not been transcribed. The available categories do not represent all gender identities or sexual orientations. The unknown partner gender makes orientation an imperfect proxy for partner configuration. No unreported group value was inferred.
