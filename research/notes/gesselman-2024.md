# Gesselman et al. (2024)

**Citation and access.** Amanda N. Gesselman, Margaret Bennett-Brown, Simon Dubé, Ellen M. Kaufman, Jessica T. Campbell, and Justin R. Garcia, “The lifelong orgasm gap: exploring age’s impact on orgasm rates,” *Sexual Medicine* 12(3), qfae042 (2024), [DOI 10.1093/sexmed/qfae042](https://doi.org/10.1093/sexmed/qfae042). [Publisher page](https://academic.oup.com/smoa/article/12/3/qfae042/7702123), [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38957591/), and [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11217024/) consulted 2026-11-02. The PMC fetch route showed a browser check, but the article opened in a headless browser.

**Question and design.** Secondary analysis of eight cross-sectional Singles in America surveys collected 2015–2023, asking whether age is associated with self-estimated orgasm rate during sexual intercourse and whether gender/orientation patterns differ across age groups. These are independent survey snapshots, not a longitudinal follow-up of people as they age.

**Sample.** Final analytic N = 24,752 U.S. adults ages 18–100. The publisher text reports 13,127 women and 11,625 men. The analysis uses five age groups: 18–24, 25–34, 35–49, 50–64, and 65+. The survey’s gender and orientation categories are limited to those reported in its instrument.

**Measure and observed findings.** The article gives the item as: “When having sexual intercourse in general, what percentage of the time do you usually have an orgasm?” with responses from 0% to 100%. The abstract says the age association was small and that men reported higher rates in all five age groups. It gives ranges across groups of 70%–85% for men and 46%–58% for women; these ranges do not assign a value to any specific age group. Table 2 gives overall regression results and Table 3 gives within-age-group coefficients. No CSV was created from the ranges or regression coefficients because they are not interchangeable with age-specific observed rates.

**Limits.** The article relies on self-report and cross-sectional data. Orgasm during sexual intercourse is a narrower prompt than broader sexual intimacy, and it cannot be compared as though it were the same variable as Frederick’s or Armstrong’s outcome. The study cannot establish how an individual’s rate changes with age. Verify any figure-derived age values at the original figure before plotting.

**Per-age values check (2026-11-09).** The PMC full text has one figure (participant exclusions) and no table of mean orgasm rate by age group and gender. Per-age information is limited to the abstract ranges (men 70%–85%, women 46%–58%; men 22–30 higher, unit ambiguous) and Table 3 within-age regression coefficients for gender: 18–24 b = −20.52 (95% CI −23.96 to −17.09); 25–34 −25.29 (−28.18 to −22.39); 35–49 −24.61 (−26.31 to −20.91); 50–64 −25.33 (−28.49 to −22.18); 65+ −22.44 (−27.93 to −16.95). These come from models with income, survey year, orientation, and gender × orientation terms, so they are adjusted model coefficients, not raw men-minus-women gaps; do not plot them as observed percentage-point gaps without checking the coding. The overall mean (65% of intercourse occasions) is a different measure from Frederick's 65% of heterosexual women who usually/always orgasm; do not juxtapose them as the same number.

**Image exploration (2026-11-09).** The student chose these model results for concept exploration and then a wide, aligned-interval Processing test. Table 3's five gender coefficients and printed intervals are transcribed separately in `data/raw/gesselman-2024-age-model.csv`.
